## 1. Convergence-point taxonomy

A convergence point is a place where a stakeholder's history/posture intersects the deal. Tag each with **type**, **evidence** (with source), and **implication** (what we do about it).

| # | Type | What to look for | Implication / play |
|---|---|---|---|
| CP-1 | **VTEX affinity** | Prior VTEX implementation, certification, or role at a VTEX customer/partner | Warm internal advocate; lower education cost; ask them to reference their prior VTEX outcome |
| CP-2 | **Competitor incumbency** | Deep tenure building/running a competitor platform (see §2) | Known comparison lens; pre-empt their objections; counter-position on VTEX's native OMS/marketplace/unified model and lower TCO |
| CP-3 | **Ex-competitor employee** | Previously worked *for* a competitor vendor/agency | Strong platform loyalty likely; expect feature-parity scrutiny; don't overclaim |
| CP-4 | **Built the incumbent** | Designed/owns the system being replaced | Replatform threatens their work → political/emotional risk; give them a leadership role in the new build |
| CP-5 | **Build-it-ourselves track record** | From an engineering-heavy org / in-house platform program / prior custom build | Real competitor may be "build internally / do nothing"; quantify time-to-value and opportunity cost of building |
| CP-6 | **Shared history with VTEX** | Overlapping past employer, mutual connections, prior engagement with a VTEX team member | Warm path — route the intro through the shared contact |
| CP-7 | **Domain/vertical match** | Career in the deal's vertical (QSR, marketplace, B2B, cross-border, luxury) | Tailor proof points and demo scenarios to what they already value |
| CP-8 | **Digital-transformation mandate** | Hired/promoted to drive digital, "100% digital", replatform, or growth P&L | Personal win tied to the project's success → high champion potential |
| CP-9 | **Recent joiner / new mandate** | In role < ~12 months, especially senior | Wants an early visible win; open to change; move fast |
| CP-10 | **Governance gatekeeper** | Owns global/regional platform standards or a mandated stack | Can veto a local deal; needs endorsement or non-objection early |

Multiple convergence points can stack on one person (e.g., CP-1 + CP-8 = strong champion; CP-2 + CP-4 = handle-with-care skeptic).

## 2. Competitor-platform affinity reference

When reading career history, flag which commerce platform(s) each person has worked with. Use this to set CP-1/CP-2/CP-3 and to prepare counter-positioning.

| Platform | Aliases / tells | Counter-position emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **VTEX** | VTEX IO, FastStore, VTEX marketplace/OMS | (affinity — reinforce) |
| **Salesforce Commerce Cloud** | Demandware, SFCC, SFRA | Commerce focus; unified platform; less add-on dependency; native marketplace |
| **commercetools** | CT, MACH, "composable" purist | Lower assembly complexity; faster go-live; native commerce capabilities vs. multi-vendor stitching |
| **Shopify / Shopify Plus** | Shopify, Plus | Enterprise B2B/B2C depth; native OMS/marketplace; multi-country; extensibility |
| **Adobe Commerce** | Magento, AEM | Lower operational overhead; cloud-native; no heavy custom stack to maintain |
| **SAP Commerce Cloud** | Hybris, SAP CX | Faster implementation; lower integration complexity; modern composable extensibility |
| **Mirakl** | marketplace-only | Unified commerce + marketplace in one platform vs. bolt-on marketplace; native storefront/OMS |
| **Oracle Commerce** | ATG, Oracle CX | Modern cloud-native architecture; TCO; speed |
| **BigCommerce / Elastic Path / custom** | in-house, headless build | Time-to-value; maintainability; total cost of ownership vs. building/owning it |

Keep it fair: a competitor background is a **lens to anticipate**, not proof of hostility.

## 3. Champion signals vs. blocker signals

**Champion signals (advocate potential):**
- Personal win tied to project success (CP-8 mandate, growth P&L, replatform they'll be credited for)
- VTEX affinity (CP-1) or positive prior experience with our approach
- Initiated the outreach or is driving the evaluation day-to-day
- Publicly advocates composable / headless / unified commerce / speed
- Recent joiner wanting an early win (CP-9)
- Has both the *will* (supportive) and the *way* (influence) to sell internally

A real champion needs **influence + a personal stake + access to the economic buyer**. Enthusiasm without influence is a coach, not a champion.

**Blocker / skeptic signals:**
- Built or owns the incumbent (CP-4) — replatform is a threat
- Deep competitor loyalty (CP-2/CP-3)
- Governance gatekeeper enforcing a different standard (CP-10)
- Build-it-ourselves conviction (CP-5)
- Procurement/finance whose incentive is cost/risk reduction, not the solution

Blockers are often convertible: give CP-4 a leadership role in the new architecture; give CP-10 the compliance story; give CP-5 the build-vs-buy math.

## 4. DMU role definitions

| Role | Definition | How to engage |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic Buyer** | Controls budget; final yes/no | Executive value case: GMV growth, TCO, time-to-value |
| **Technical Decision-Maker / Gatekeeper** | Owns architecture fit and technical approval | Architecture depth, integration, security, migration realism |
| **Champion** | Influential internal advocate with a personal stake | Arm them to sell internally; co-build the business case |
| **Coach** | Gives us information/guidance but lacks authority | Nurture; use for intel and internal navigation |
| **Influencer** | Shapes opinion without deciding (e.g., peer, analyst-minded) | Reference stories, thought leadership |
| **End-User** | Operates the platform day-to-day | Usability, ops workflows, enablement |
| **Blocker** | Can slow or veto | Convert or neutralize early; don't ignore |
| **Procurement** | Runs the commercial/compliance process | Clear commercials, references, risk answers |

One person can hold two roles (e.g., Champion + Technical Decision-Maker).

## 5. Influence, support stance, and champion score

**Influence level** — ability to move the decision:
- **High** — economic buyer, governance owner, or the decisive technical authority
- **Medium** — shapes requirements, owns a workstream, senior working-level
- **Low** — input/execution only

**Support stance** — orientation toward VTEX:
- **Champion** (actively sells us) → **Supporter** → **Neutral** → **Skeptic** → **Blocker**

**Champion score (0–5)** — quick heuristic, +1 each:
1. High or Medium influence
2. A personal win tied to project success (CP-8 / CP-9 / growth mandate)
3. VTEX affinity or positive prior experience (CP-1)
4. Access to the economic buyer
5. Supportive or Champion stance today

Score 4–5 = cultivate as primary champion. 2–3 = coach/nurture. 0–1 = not a champion (may still be an influencer or a convert target).

**The grid that drives strategy** — plot Influence (y) × Support (x):
- High influence + Supportive/Champion → **invest**, arm them, meet often
- High influence + Skeptic/Blocker → **convert or neutralize** early
- Low influence + Supportive → **coach**, use for intel
- Low influence + Skeptic → **monitor**, don't over-rotate

## 6. Worked examples

**Example A — the likely champion**
History: E-commerce Manager hired 8 months ago to lead a replatform; previously ran D2C on VTEX at a prior retailer; initiated the vendor outreach.
Convergence: CP-1 (VTEX affinity) + CP-8 (transformation mandate) + CP-9 (recent joiner).
Read: Influence Medium, Stance Champion, Champion score 5. **Primary champion — arm them and give them the internal business case.**

**Example B — the handle-with-care gatekeeper**
History: Solutions Architect who designed the current in-house platform on a competitor stack; long tenure.
Convergence: CP-2 (competitor incumbency) + CP-4 (built the incumbent).
Read: Influence High (technical veto), Stance Skeptic. **Convert: give them ownership of the new architecture; pre-empt the competitor comparison; don't overclaim.**

**Example C — the warm-path economic buyer**
History: Regional Director of Technology, digital commerce + P&L; 2nd-degree on LinkedIn with two VTEX colleagues as mutuals.
Convergence: CP-6 (shared history / mutual connections) + CP-10 (governance owner) + CP-8 (growth P&L).
Read: Influence High, Stance Neutral. **Route a warm intro through the mutual connections to secure endorsement before the local process hardens.**
